Hook math

A coat rail with a hook too few, hooks a forehead too low, hooks that block the door they hide behind - all bought by eye. Hook math turns the rail length and a labeled pitch into the hook count, turns the tallest hanger and a labeled factor into the hook line, and turns the space and a labeled projection into the clearance margin. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Row plan - the rail length at a labeled pitch a hook becomes the hook count and the spare centimeters.
  • Line plan - the tallest hanger's height at a labeled factor becomes the hook line on the wall.
  • Depth plan - the space minus a labeled hook projection becomes the clearance margin.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 15 cm of rail for each hook, a hook line at 105% of the tallest hanger's height, a 12 cm hook projection - all illustrative defaults you can change. Walls, households and hooks all vary. This is planning arithmetic, not a mounting instruction: measure the real wall and follow the hook maker's fixing sheet.
